About

Mengyuan (Marvin) Li is an Assistant Professor in the Thomas Lord Department of Computer Science at the University of Southern California, where he leads SEPT Lab (Security, Privacy, and Trust). Before joining USC in 2024, he was a postdoctoral researcher at MIT CSAIL (2022–2024), working with Prof. Mengjia Yan. He received his Ph.D. in Computer Science and Engineering from The Ohio State University in 2022, advised by Prof. Yinqian Zhang. His research on AMD SEV led to several AMD security bulletins and CVEs, and his paper CROSSLINE received the Best Paper Award Runner-Up at ACM CCS 2021.

Research

My group pursues two research thrusts:

  1. Uncovering, understanding, and defending against system and hardware vulnerabilities in cloud systems, CPUs, GPUs, and AI systems.
  2. Co-designing software and hardware for secure and efficient AI systems.
Research directions

  • Verifiable AI

    Evidence that an AI result came from the claimed model and computation, produced without revealing sensitive data. SEPT Lab also maintains verifiable.pro, a public resource with news, explainers, and guides on verifiable AI.

    Topics: zero-knowledge verification · LLM inference · model oversight

  • TEE and Confidential Computing

    Practical hardware-isolated execution: efficient and secure TEE-based systems for cloud and AI workloads, from oblivious memory inside VM-based TEEs to confidential multi-GPU machine learning.

    Topics: AMD SEV-SNP · Intel SGX · confidential VMs and GPUs · ORAM

  • System and Hardware Vulnerabilities

    Design flaws and side channels that break the security guarantees of systems and hardware, found and disclosed to vendors such as AMD.

    Topics: side channels · SEV-SNP · macOS sandboxing · GPUs

  • AI Agent Security

    Observing and protecting agent execution with TEEs and runtime monitoring, so that agents can be trusted with sensitive data on shared infrastructure.

    Topics: runtime monitoring · tool isolation · agent memory

Recruiting for Fall 2027

PhD students

For Fall 2027, Yue Zhao, Ruolin Li, and I are recruiting up to six PhD students together through a research cluster that spans our departments. Ruolin Li works on autonomous systems and Yue Zhao on trustworthy AI, while my group focuses on systems and security. Our shared goal is intelligent systems that remain reliable and secure when they are deployed in the real world. Students in the cluster can work with more than one of us and pursue questions that cross our research areas. Co-advising is possible but not required.

To apply, submit your application through USC Viterbi Graduate Admission (PhD application information). If you are interested in working with me, email mengyuanli@usc.edu with “[SEPT PhD]” in the subject line.

Research collaboration

Current USC MS and undergraduate students are welcome to work on research projects in SEPT Lab, and students at other universities can apply to collaborate with us. If you are interested, email mengyuanli@usc.edu with “[SEPT Research]” in the subject line.

Teaching

    CSCI 530: Security Systems

    Graduate course on protecting computer networks and systems with cryptography, authentication and authorization, and intrusion detection and response. A lab component gives students hands-on experience with these mechanisms.

  • Fall 2025, Fall 2026

    CSCI 699: Understanding and Identifying Side-Channel Threats in Cloud and LLM Systems

    Graduate seminar on side-channel attacks across multi-tenant cloud platforms, TEEs, GPUs, and LLM services.

  • Spring 2025, Spring 2026

    CSCI 699: Confidential Computing: Protecting Your Data on Cloud GPUs and CPUs

    Graduate course on trusted execution environments, confidential VMs, and confidential GPUs, together with related privacy techniques such as federated learning, secure multi-party computation, and fully homomorphic encryption.
